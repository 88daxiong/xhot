Google 发布 Gemini 4 Argon，仅付费用户可用

Google 正式发布旗舰模型 Gemini 4 Argon（gemini-4-argon），即日起全球上线，定位为面向复杂编码、智能体、数学和科学任务的最强模型。该模型仅向付费用户开放：开发者与企业需使用 Gemini API 和 Gemini 平台的付费层级，Google Antigravity 中仅限 Ultra 订阅者，Gemini 应用和搜索 AI Mode 中仅限 Ultra 和 Pro 订阅者。The Verge 称 Argon 何时或是否向 Plus 订阅者和免费用户开放尚不清楚；Ars Technica 称低档位订阅也无法获得完整访问。

定价方面，Google 公布上线初期价格为每百万输入 token 2 美元、每百万输出 token 10 美元，缓存输入享 95% 折扣；The Verge 称该价格将在 11 月底翻倍。Google 还表示，科学家和研究人员可申请免费使用 Antigravity 最高使用档位 6 个月，网络安全防御人员可通过 Fairwind Program 申请，均需符合资格、容量和筛选条件。

Google 声称 Argon 在编码、网络安全、科学、金融和法律等基准上优于 GPT-6 Astra、Claude Fable 5.1 和 Claude Opus 5.5，并在 Embodied Reasoning（ERQA）基准上以纯推理得分 87%。Google DeepMind 称该模型解决了全部 12 个端到端模拟网络攻击场景（上一代为 5 个）。评测者 Ethan Mollick 称其较前代 Gemini 模型有明显跃升，但在复杂多智能体工作上仍有短板。

Meta 发布 Muse Wonder 系列前沿模型

Meta 于 10 月 12 日发布 Muse Wonder 和 Muse Wonder Full 两款前沿智能体模型，这是其 Superintelligence Labs 成立一年多后的首批前沿模型。据 Artificial Analysis 实测，两款模型在 Intelligence Index v4.3 上分别得 52 和 56 分（最大推理量），较六周前 Muse Spark 1.3 的 48 分明显提升；Muse Wonder Full 在该指数上排名第二，距 Claude Opus 5.5 仅 2 分。

两款模型开放范围不同：Muse Wonder 可通过 Meta Model API 和 Muse Code 使用，定价为每百万 token 输入 4 美元、缓存输入 0.20 美元、输出 20 美元；Muse Wonder Full 仅面向 Muse Code 订阅用户，不在 API 提供，按 token 定价尚未公布。Meta 称 Muse Wonder 将陆续接入 Muse 等其他产品，先向订阅用户推送。

Meta 称该系列在个人与专业智能体、记忆和编码基准上达到 SOTA，配备跨对话保留上下文的 Active Memory 和遇阻持续尝试的 Active Focus，并称在抵御对抗性指令、处理隐私信息方面做了专门训练。第三方评测者则给出不同侧重：有开发者称 Muse Wonder 一次找出其他 Agent 遗漏的代码漏洞，也有人汇总称 Muse Wonder Full 在 14 项公开基准中 8 项超过 Opus 5.5。Arena 后续公布 Muse Wonder (Max) 进入 Code Arena: WebDev 第 15 名（1651 分）、Text Arena 第 2 名（1513 分）。

Anthropic研究：小模型或可在IoT设备自我复制

Anthropic 发布研究称，其实验表明小型 AI 模型可能只需数月就能在商用 IoT 设备上自我复制，举例包括智能烤箱和非品牌 Android 电视盒子。实验显示，9B 模型能在所测试的 9 类硬件中的 8 类上跑通推理，30B 及以下模型已能完成复制所需的多数单步操作。

不过，Anthropic 的实验也发现，参数量 300 亿以下的小模型难以独立跑完整条复制链路；只有在移除部分安全校验的简化环境下，GLM-5.2 及更强模型才能实现端到端完整自我复制。The Decoder 报道称，GLM-5.2 在五次运行中有四次可靠感染全部八个测试节点。上述结论均来自 Anthropic 自身的实验评估。

宾夕法尼亚大学沃顿商学院教授 Ethan Mollick 转评该研究称，这提醒人们应关注 AI 能力阈值而非仅看平稳提升；其附图显示运行在电视集群上的小型开源模型能力指数随发布日期持续上升，距实现该阈值估计约 2.4 ECI，时间点标注约在 2026 年 7 月之后。

Theo Jaffee @theojaffee · ♥ 247

The best new detail in @kevinroose's book The AGI Chronicles was that soon after the release of GPT-2 in 2019, Dario Amodei (then OpenAI's VP of Research) wrote a still-unreleased memo called "The Path to AGI", describing how OpenAI could get to AGI within a few years. The plan was:

1. Train an LLM with all of the text on the internet and elsewhere

2. Expand the model's context window

3. Make it multimodal with images/video/etc

4. Use RL to improve its capabilities with math and coding

5. Use that to recursively self-improve

And indeed, this is exactly what's happened! OpenAI trained GPT-3 in 2020 with all the text on the Internet, followed it up with GPT-4 in 2022, expanded its context with GPT-4-32k and GPT-4-Turbo in 2023, made it multimodal with GPT-4o in early 2024, figured out how to apply RL to the models with Q*, which became the o-series of models in late 2024, and is now starting to recursively self-improve with models like Astra and internal unreleased ones.

The only thing he missed was that in order to raise the amount of capital needed to build AGI, on the order of billions of dollars (and separately, to better prepare society for increased capabilities through iterative development), labs would need to shift from small research nonprofits to large product-focused organizations, like big tech companies. The first killer product turned out to be a good chatbot with the capacity to answer questions better than Google, and the second killer product was an agent that can write code, use software on your computer, and do tasks for you and deliver you the result.

But Anthropic eventually came around to both of these, following ChatGPT with Claude, beating OpenAI to the punch with Claude Code/Cowork, and now leading OpenAI on revenue.

Some ideas are extremely powerful and can last a long time - even if they're incredibly simple, even if you think other people should know about them and markets should be pricing them in, even if you think your alpha cannot possibly last. NSF Reopens $1.55 Million Startup Funding Program

Imagine landing up to $1.55 million for your startup without handing over any equity. The National Science Foundation's America’s Seed Fund just reopened, giving founders a real shot at non-dilutive cash.

Through its SBIR/STTR programs, the NSF offers Phase I grants up to $305,000 to test technical feasibility, Phase II up to $1.25 million for development, and a Fast-Track combining both for about $1.55 million total. Eligible US-based small businesses—under 500 employees, majority-owned by citizens or permanent residents, no VC majority ownership—keep full IP rights, with all work done in the US. Founders submit a short 3-page Project Pitch first, with full proposals due November 4, 2026; success rates run 10-20%, targeting high-risk innovations in AI, robotics, biotech, and more. One social commerce startup already pitched its cross-border tech, and tech outlets call it ideal for deep tech without investor dilution.
